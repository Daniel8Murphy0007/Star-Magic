# Paper #2: GW190425 Mass Gap Interpretation via UQFF Whitepaper

## Abstract
This whitepaper presents an analysis of the mass gap interpretation of the gravitational wave event GW190425, utilizing the Uncertainty Quantification Framework for Fields (UQFF).

## Introduction
The mass gap problem refers to the observation that there are no known stellar remnants (black holes or neutron stars) with masses between approximately 2.5 and 5 solar masses. The discovery of GW190425 has ignited further investigation into this phenomenon.

### GW190425 Discovery
GW190425 was detected by LIGO and Virgo on April 25, 2019, and has mass components suggesting new insights into the formation and evolution of compact binary objects.

## UQFF Theoretical Framework
The UQFF allows for an in-depth analysis of gravitational wave signals by considering damping factors and mass gap components. The analysis discussed here involves both Black Hole (BH) and Neutron Star (NS) hypotheses.

### Mass Gap Component Analysis
Initial results, including chirp mass computations, are presented as follows:
- **Chirp Mass:** 1.44 solar masses 
- **Component Masses:**
  - m1 = 2.52 solar masses 
  - m2 = 1.12 solar masses 
- **Distance:** 159 Mpc 
- **UQFF Combined Factor:** 0.5297 
- **Amplitude Reduction:** 47.0% 
- **SNR Observed:** 12.9 
- **UQFF SNR:** 3.6
