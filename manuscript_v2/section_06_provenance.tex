\section{Provenance and parameter locking}
\label{sec:provenance}

Section~\ref{sec:primitives} listed the nine truly-independent
primitives and asserted that they are ``locked'' --- calibrated once,
never adjusted to fit downstream observations. This section
documents how each primitive earned that locked status, presents the
A++--C provenance grading rubric used to audit primitive sources,
and gives two worked examples of \emph{provenance audits performed
to date that resulted in a primitive being demoted to ``derivative''}
(PAPER\_1521 for $D_{\text{BSFG}}$, PAPER\_1522 for $K_{\text{MEX}}$).

\subsection{The provenance grading rubric}
\label{sec:provenance_rubric}

The framework's nine primitives are audited against a five-tier
grading rubric documented in \texttt{PROVENANCE\_AUDIT.md}
(bundled with the package). The rubric grades each primitive on the
quality of the evidence that fixes its value.

\centering
\caption{The A++ to C provenance-grading rubric for UQFF primitives.}
\label{tab:provenance_rubric}
\begin{tabular}{l l p{8cm}}
\toprule
\textbf{Grade} & \textbf{Status} & \textbf{Meaning} \\
\midrule
A++ & Structural integer & Locked by representation theory or group theory; cannot be perturbed continuously (e.g., $\mathrm{SO}_5 = 10$, $|A_5| = 60$) \\

\subsection{The locking discipline in practice}
\label{sec:locking_in_practice}

Three concrete mechanisms enforce that a locked primitive's value
does not silently drift downstream:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Fidelity-gate assertions.} \texttt{uqff\_fidelity\_tests.py}
        asserts the canonical numerical value of every locked
        primitive on every commit. Any change requires a deliberate
        edit to both the calculator and the gate, plus a SESSION\_LOG
        entry; any drift causes the gate to fail and blocks any
        release tag from publishing.
  \item \textbf{Single-file calculator constraint.} The fact that
        \texttt{uqff\_pure\_calculator.py} is a single file (see
        Sec.~\ref{sec:software_architecture}) prevents the common
        ``two-file drift'' antipattern in which a constant's value
        differs between a definitions file and a downstream consumer.
        All primitives are defined once at the top of the file (lines
        $\sim$140--160) and referenced symbolically thereafter.
  \item \textbf{C++ cross-language verification.} Approximately 632
        closures are re-implemented in \texttt{uqff\_exact\_closures.cpp}
        with the primitives hardcoded as compile-time constants. The
        Python-vs-C++ cross-check (Sec.~\ref{sec:cpp_crosscheck})
        catches any case in which the two implementations disagree
        on a primitive's value.
\end{enumerate}

The combination of these three mechanisms makes silent re-tuning of
a primitive practically impossible: it would require simultaneous
edits to the Python calculator, the C++ reference, the fidelity
gate, and at least one whitepaper, with all four changes coordinated
to not trigger any one of the other audits.

\subsection{Worked example: PAPER\_1521 demotes $D_{\text{BSFG}}$ to derivative}
\label{sec:paper_1521_worked}

In April--May 2026, the framework's \texttt{LOCKED\_PRIMITIVES} table
listed $D_{\text{BSFG}} = 6$ as an independent integer primitive,
on the historical grounds that it appeared in the
$(D_{\text{crit}}/D_{\text{BSFG}})^2$ dimensional factor of the
DPM-buoyancy closure (Sec.~\ref{sec:yang_mills},
Eq.~\ref{eq:ym_dpm_closure}) without a documented derivation from
the other integer primitives. A targeted provenance audit
(SESSION\_LOG entry 2026-06-18) noted that
$26 - 2 \cdot 10 = 6$ is the immediate algebraic consequence of
$D_{\text{crit}} = 26$ and $\mathrm{SO}_5 = 10$, both of which are
A++ grade.

PAPER\_1521 \cite{Murphy_PAPER_1521} formalizes the reduction:
$D_{\text{BSFG}} = D_{\text{crit}} - 2\,\mathrm{SO}_5$ is an exact
integer identity, derived from two A++ primitives, and therefore
$D_{\text{BSFG}}$ does not require independent locking. The
calculator added a dedicated \texttt{d\_bsfg\_derived\_from\_d\_crit}
dispatch that the fidelity gate asserts on every commit. The
\texttt{LOCKED\_PRIMITIVES} table still carries $D_{\text{BSFG}} = 6$
at its canonical value for interface stability, but the
truly-independent primitive count dropped from 10 to 9 as a result.

\subsection{Worked example: PAPER\_1522 demotes $K_{\text{MEX}}$ to derivative}
\label{sec:paper_1522_worked}

The same audit cycle examined $K_{\text{MEX}} = 25/12$, which had
been carried as an independent rational primitive on the historical
grounds that it appeared in the cosmological-constant closure
(Sec.~\ref{sec:lambda}, Eq.~\ref{eq:lambda_uqff}) and in the
$m_d/m_u$ quark mass ratio (Sec.~\ref{sec:sm_spectrum}). PAPER\_1522
\cite{Murphy_PAPER_1522} demonstrated that
\[
K_{\text{MEX}} \;=\; \Phi_{5/6}\cdot\frac{\mathrm{SO}_5}{D_{\text{phys}}}
\;=\; \frac{5}{6}\cdot\frac{10}{4} \;=\; \frac{25}{12}
\]
follows from $\Phi_{5/6} = 5/6$ (PAPER\_1522's own derivation from
$D_{\text{BSFG}}$), $\mathrm{SO}_5 = 10$, and $D_{\text{phys}} = 4$.
The reduction takes the truly-independent count from 10 to 9 (after
the PAPER\_1521 reduction had already brought it from 11 to 10).

The combined PAPER\_1521 + PAPER\_1522 effect dropped the
truly-independent primitive count from \textbf{11} (the count in
effect through 2026-Q1) to \textbf{9} (the count used in the BIC
calculation of Sec.~\ref{sec:bic}). The BIC argument
$\Delta\mathrm{BIC} = 17 \cdot \ln 253 = 94.1$ was strengthened
correspondingly: the 17-parameter advantage over SM+$\Lambda$CDM
includes the contribution from these two proven reductions.

\subsection{The remaining open questions ($\mathrm{SSq}, S_{26}, \omega_{\text{SCm}}$)}
\label{sec:open_provenance}

Three quantities currently carry a C-grade provenance in
\texttt{PROVENANCE\_AUDIT.md}:

\paragraph{$\mathrm{SSq} = 0.57$.}
The state-space-square parameter. Used in $S_{26}^{(3)}$ regularization
(Sec.~\ref{sec:holmlid}) and in the cosmological-constant closure
chain. Currently locked at $0.57$ on historical grounds; a Q3
analysis (SESSION\_LOG entry 2026-06-08) raised the open question
whether $\mathrm{SSq}$ might be derivable from $F_{\text{TRZ}}$ and
$\Phi_{\text{res}}$ via a transcendental identity. The work is open.

\paragraph{$S_{26} = 1.453162$ and $S_{26}^{(3)} \simeq 1.453 \times 10^{26}$.}
The 26-level Ramanujan series at $\mathrm{SSq} = 0.57$. Locked at
the numerically-evaluated value; the question is whether the
specific functional form
($S_{26}(\mathrm{SSq}) = \prod_{k} f_k(\mathrm{SSq})$ for some
$f_k$) is itself reducible.

\paragraph{$\omega_{\text{SCm}} = 1.25\,\mathrm{THz}$.}
The SCm phonon carrier frequency. Calibrated to the Holmlid 630\,eV
KER via PAPER\_1133; the open question is whether
$\omega_{\text{SCm}}$ might be derivable from $\rho_{\text{SCm}}$
and the integer primitives via dimensional analysis on a candidate
dispersion relation.

If any of these three prove derivative on subsequent analysis, the
truly-independent primitive count drops below 9 and the BIC
argument of Sec.~\ref{sec:bic} strengthens. The conservative
posture taken in this manuscript is to retain them as nine
truly-independent primitives until the demotion proofs exist.

\subsection{Summary}

The framework's parameter-locking is enforced by a triple-layer
discipline (fidelity gate, single-file calculator, C++ cross-check)
that makes silent re-tuning impractical. Two primitives that had
historically been carried as independent ($D_{\text{BSFG}}$ and
$K_{\text{MEX}}$) have been proven derivative in 2026, dropping the
truly-independent count from 11 to 9. Three further quantities
remain open audit candidates, any of which would further reduce the
truly-independent count if proven derivative. The
parameter-economy claim of Sec.~\ref{sec:bic} is therefore an
upper bound, not a tight estimate, on the framework's free-parameter
count.
