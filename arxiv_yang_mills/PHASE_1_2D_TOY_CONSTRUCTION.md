## 1. Why 2D first: the Hairer template

The Clay Yang-Mills existence and mass gap problem requires a 4D construction. This construction is universally acknowledged as extremely hard — Hairer's 2022 Fields-Medal-follow-up work on Langevin dynamics for the 2D Yang-Mills measure and his 2024 *Inventiones Mathematicae* result on stochastic quantisation of Yang-Mills-Higgs in 3D established a template that starts in 2D and works up.

Constructive quantum field theory in 2D is a solved subject in a way it is not in higher dimensions:
- **Free scalar fields on** $\mathbb{R}^{1,1}$ **satisfy the Wightman axioms** (Wightman, Streater 1964).
- $\phi^4_2$ **is rigorously constructed and Wightman-compliant** (Glimm-Jaffe 1968-1970).
- **Osterwalder-Schrader reconstruction** from Euclidean 2D QFT to Wightman theory is established.
- **Reflection positivity** techniques work cleanly in 2D.

The strategy for the UQFF Phase 1 construction is therefore: **treat the SCm/UA substrate in 2D as a specific quantum field theory in the Glimm-Jaffe tradition, verify Wightman axioms via the standard 2D constructive-QFT toolkit, and prove $E_{\text{crack}}^{(2D)} > 0$ as a spectral gap.**

If Phase 1 succeeds (in the sense of publication in a math-physics journal), Phase 2 (3D via regularity structures, following Hairer 2024) and Phase 3 (4D principal result) become concrete follow-on programmes.

## 2. Definitions and notation

### 2.1 2D Minkowski space

$$
\mathbb{R}^{1,1} := \{ (t, x) : t, x \in \mathbb{R} \}
$$

with metric $\eta = \text{diag}(-1, +1)$. The connected Poincaré group is $\mathcal{P}_+^\uparrow := \text{ISO}(1,1)_+^\uparrow$, generated by boosts $\Lambda_\beta$, translations $T_a$, and their compositions.

Test-function space: $\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{1,1})$, Schwartz functions on 2D spacetime.

### 2.2 The UQFF 2D substrate (definitional)

The full 4D SCm/UA substrate is characterized by:

- Resonance ratio $[\text{SSq}] = 0.57$
- The 9-primitive lattice $(D_{\text{phys}}, D_{\text{crit}}, N_{\text{ch}}, \text{SO}_5, A_5, \rho_{\text{SCm}}, \beta_i, \Phi_{\text{res}}, F_{\text{TRZ}})$

For the 2D toy, we **project onto the 2D reduction**:

**Definition 2.1** (2D SCm/UA substrate). The 2D SCm/UA substrate is the pair $(\phi_{\text{SCm}}, \phi_{\text{UA}})$ of real-valued distributional fields on $\mathbb{R}^{1,1}$ with actions

$$
S_{\text{SCm}}[\phi_{\text{SCm}}] = \int d^2x \, \left[ \frac{1}{2}(\partial\phi_{\text{SCm}})^2 + \frac{1}{2} m_{\text{SCm}}^2 \phi_{\text{SCm}}^2 + V_{\text{SCm}}(\phi_{\text{SCm}}) \right]
$$

$$
S_{\text{UA}}[\phi_{\text{UA}}] = \int d^2x \, \left[ \frac{1}{2}(\partial\phi_{\text{UA}})^2 + \frac{1}{2} m_{\text{UA}}^2 \phi_{\text{UA}}^2 + V_{\text{UA}}(\phi_{\text{UA}}) \right]
$$

with attraction-coupling

$$
S_{\text{int}}[\phi_{\text{SCm}}, \phi_{\text{UA}}] = \int d^2x \, \left[ -\lambda \, \phi_{\text{SCm}} \phi_{\text{UA}} - \kappa \, \phi_{\text{SCm}}^2 \phi_{\text{UA}}^2 \right]
$$

where:

- $m_{\text{SCm}}, m_{\text{UA}} \geq 0$ are the substrate mass parameters (from the 4D UQFF primitives via dimensional reduction — see Gap G-2.1 below)
- $\lambda > 0$ is the SCm-UA attraction coupling (associated with the DPM formation force)
- $\kappa > 0$ is the quartic interaction (associated with the crossing-zone repulsion FUBi vs. FUBii)
- $V_{\text{SCm}}, V_{\text{UA}}$ are polynomial self-interaction potentials whose forms are yet-to-be-fixed by the 4D-to-2D reduction

The **2D SCm/UA action** is $S_{\text{2D}} := S_{\text{SCm}} + S_{\text{UA}} + S_{\text{int}}$.

**Gap G-2.1** (dimensional reduction from 4D to 2D). The exact form of $V_{\text{SCm}}, V_{\text{UA}}$ from the 4D UQFF ledger primitives requires specifying the compactification of two spatial dimensions. The natural choice is compactification on a torus $T^2$ of characteristic length equal to the DPM coherence length, but the resulting Kaluza-Klein tower on the 2D residual theory needs explicit computation. **What needs to be done**: compute the 4D-to-2D dimensional reduction with the KK modes explicitly, verify the residual 2D action has the form of a $P(\phi)_2$ or $\phi^4_2$-like theory, identify $V_{\text{SCm}}, V_{\text{UA}}$ explicitly. **Reference**: standard Kaluza-Klein reduction technique; e.g., Duff-Nilsson-Pope 1986 for the general framework.

## 3. Construction of the 2D Hilbert space

Working in Euclidean signature initially (following the Osterwalder-Schrader / Glimm-Jaffe convention), define the Euclidean action

$$
S^E_{\text{2D}} = \int d^2x_E \, \left[ \frac{1}{2}(\partial_E\phi_{\text{SCm}})^2 + \frac{1}{2}(\partial_E\phi_{\text{UA}})^2 + V_{\text{eff}}(\phi_{\text{SCm}}, \phi_{\text{UA}}) \right]
$$

with $V_{\text{eff}}$ the effective 2D potential defined by dimensional reduction.

### 3.1 Constructing the measure

Following the Glimm-Jaffe method for $\phi^4_2$:

**Proposition 3.1** (Existence of the SCm-UA 2D Euclidean measure). *Under the assumption (**Assumption A-3.1**) that the effective potential $V_{\text{eff}}$ is a semibounded polynomial in $\phi_{\text{SCm}}$ and $\phi_{\text{UA}}$ (i.e., a `P(ϕ)_2`-type interaction), there exists a Borel probability measure $\mu_{\text{SCm-UA}}$ on $\mathcal{S}'(\mathbb{R}^2)^{\oplus 2}$ (the space of pairs of tempered distributions) such that the moment functions satisfy the Osterwalder-Schrader axioms OS0-OS3.*

**Proof sketch**: The construction proceeds through the standard 2D constructive-QFT steps:
1. Approximate the interaction by a lattice cutoff at scale $\varepsilon$ and volume $|\Lambda|$.
2. Verify the interaction is semibounded (from below) via **Assumption A-3.1**.
3. Take the thermodynamic limit $|\Lambda| \to \infty$ using cluster expansion arguments.
4. Take the continuum limit $\varepsilon \to 0$ using UV-finite renormalization.
5. Verify the moment functions satisfy OS0-OS3.

The novelty in the UQFF case is the specific form of $V_{\text{eff}}$; the constructive machinery is standard Glimm-Jaffe / Fröhlich / Sokal.

**Gap G-3.1**: Assumption A-3.1 needs verification. The SCm-UA attraction is inherently attractive ($\lambda > 0$ produces the $-\lambda \phi_{\text{SCm}} \phi_{\text{UA}}$ term), which is *not* semibounded from below by itself. The quartic coupling $\kappa \phi_{\text{SCm}}^2 \phi_{\text{UA}}^2$ with $\kappa > 0$ IS semibounded and stabilizes the attractive term for large field values. **What needs to be shown**: the combined potential $V_{\text{eff}}$ is semibounded, and the region of $(\lambda, \kappa)$ parameter space where semiboundedness holds is non-empty and physically consistent with the UQFF ledger. **Reference**: Glimm-Jaffe Chapter 6 for the general framework of $P(\phi)_2$ constructive existence proofs.

### 3.2 Osterwalder-Schrader reconstruction

**Proposition 3.2** (2D SCm-UA Wightman theory). *Under Proposition 3.1's Osterwalder-Schrader measure, the Osterwalder-Schrader reconstruction theorem produces a Wightman-compliant 2D quantum field theory with Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}_{\text{2D}}$, cyclic vacuum vector $\Omega_{\text{2D}}$, and field operators $\phi_{\text{SCm}}^{(2D)}(f), \phi_{\text{UA}}^{(2D)}(f)$ for $f \in \mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{1,1})$.*

**Proof sketch**: Direct application of the Osterwalder-Schrader reconstruction theorem (1973). Given OS0-OS3 hold for the Euclidean measure of Proposition 3.1, the Wightman functions are recovered by analytic continuation, and the GNS construction yields $(\mathcal{H}_{\text{2D}}, \Omega_{\text{2D}}, \phi_{\text{SCm}}^{(2D)}, \phi_{\text{UA}}^{(2D)})$ satisfying W0-W4.

**Gap G-3.2**: The precise identification of $\Omega_{\text{2D}}$ with $\theta_{\text{vacuum}}$ (the 4D UQFF vacuum) after dimensional reduction requires making explicit how the emergent-spacetime aspect of the 4D theory reduces in 2D. **What needs to be shown**: $\Omega_{\text{2D}}$ is unique (no ground-state degeneracy in the 2D reduction) and is the physically-motivated $\theta_{\text{vacuum}}^{(2D)}$. **Reference**: Streater-Wightman, *PCT, Spin & Statistics, and All That*, Chapter 3.

## 4. The DPM vortex operator in 2D

The DPM vortex is the central UQFF entity — a Bose-Einstein-condensate ground state of the SCm/UA attraction pair. In 2D, we construct it as a composite operator.

**Definition 4.1** (2D DPM vortex operator). Define

$$
D^{(2D)}(f) := \int d^2x \, f(x) \, \phi_{\text{SCm}}(x) \phi_{\text{UA}}(x)
$$

for test functions $f \in \mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{1,1})$.

**Lemma 4.2** (DPM operator existence). *For polynomial $V_{\text{eff}}$ with the semiboundedness of Assumption A-3.1, $D^{(2D)}(f)$ is a well-defined operator on a dense domain in $\mathcal{H}_{\text{2D}}$ for each $f$, with appropriate regularity requirements on the composite $\phi_{\text{SCm}} \phi_{\text{UA}}$.*

**Proof sketch**: In 2D, the product of two free scalar fields at coincident points is Wick-ordered to yield a well-defined composite operator (unlike in 4D, where UV divergences require more delicate renormalization). Follow Glimm-Jaffe Chapter 12 on composite fields in 2D.

**Gap G-4.1**: The full regularity requirements on $D^{(2D)}(f)$ (specifically, whether $f$ needs to be Schwartz or can be extended to more general distributions) require explicit computation of the 2-point and 4-point functions of the composite. **Reference**: Fredenhagen-Rehren-Seiler operator-product-expansion techniques.

## 5. Verification of Wightman axioms W0-W4

### 5.1 W0 — Hilbert space + unique vacuum

**Claim 5.1**: $(\mathcal{H}_{\text{2D}}, \Omega_{\text{2D}})$ of Proposition 3.2 satisfies W0.

**Status**: Rigorously established via Osterwalder-Schrader reconstruction, contingent on Gap G-3.1 (semiboundedness) and Gap G-3.2 (vacuum uniqueness).

### 5.2 W1 — Poincaré covariance

**Claim 5.2**: The 2D Poincaré group $\mathcal{P}_+^{\uparrow}(1,1)$ acts by unitary operators $U(a, \Lambda)$ on $\mathcal{H}_{\text{2D}}$ leaving $\Omega_{\text{2D}}$ invariant.

**Proof sketch**: The 2D Euclidean measure is $O(2)$-invariant (rotation-invariant in 2D Euclidean space). The Osterwalder-Schrader reconstruction preserves the Euclidean invariance and lifts it to the Wightman Poincaré covariance in Minkowski signature. The action $S^E_{\text{2D}}$ has manifest Euclidean invariance by construction.

**Status**: Rigorously established, contingent on Gap G-3.1 and Gap G-2.1 (that the 4D-to-2D reduction preserves the 4D Poincaré invariance in the 2D restriction).

### 5.3 W2 — Spectral condition + mass gap

**This is the principal Clay-eligible result for Phase 1.**

**Conjecture 5.3** (2D SCm-UA mass gap). *There exists $\Delta_{\text{2D}} > 0$ such that*

$$
\text{spec}(H_{\text{2D}}) \subset \{0\} \cup [\Delta_{\text{2D}}, \infty)
$$

*where $H_{\text{2D}}$ is the Hamiltonian generating time-translations on $\mathcal{H}_{\text{2D}}$, and the value $\Delta_{\text{2D}}$ is identified with the 2D reduction of $E_{\text{crack}} = \rho_{\text{SCm}} c^2 / [\text{SSq}]$.*

**Proof strategy** (proof sketch, not complete proof):

1. Establish reflection positivity of the SCm-UA 2D Euclidean measure. This is standard for scalar $P(\phi)_2$-type theories with real actions.
2. Use reflection positivity to obtain a self-adjoint transfer matrix $T$ on the reflection-positive subspace.
3. Bound the spectrum of $T$ using expansion arguments (cluster expansion, high-temperature expansion, or polymer expansion).
4. Show that the second-largest eigenvalue of $T$ is bounded away from 1, giving a positive mass gap for $H_{\text{2D}} = -\log T$.
5. Compute $\Delta_{\text{2D}}$ explicitly and match to $E_{\text{crack}}^{(2D)}$.

**Gap G-5.1** (the main mathematical challenge): Step 3 above (spectral bound on $T$) requires a controlled expansion. For weak coupling ($\lambda, \kappa$ small), this is achievable using standard cluster-expansion methods (Glimm-Jaffe Chapter 20). For the physically-relevant UQFF coupling values, the expansion may not converge, requiring stronger methods (e.g., renormalization-group flow analysis in 2D, following Kupiainen 2016).

**Reference**: Glimm-Jaffe Chapter 20 on cluster expansions; Salmhofer *Renormalization: An Introduction*, Chapters 4-5 on 2D constructive RG.

### 5.4 W3 — Local commutativity

**Claim 5.4**: The reconstructed field operators $\phi_{\text{SCm}}^{(2D)}(f), \phi_{\text{UA}}^{(2D)}(g)$ commute for $f, g$ with spacelike-separated supports.

**Proof sketch**: Standard for scalar field theories reconstructed via Osterwalder-Schrader from a reflection-positive Euclidean theory. The Euclidean $O(2)$-invariance of the measure lifts to the Wightman locality (spacelike-separated support ⇒ commutativity) via the Wick rotation.

**Status**: Rigorously established for scalar theories, standard result. Contingent on Gap G-3.1 (existence of the measure).

### 5.5 W4 — Cyclicity of vacuum

**Claim 5.5**: The polynomial algebra $\mathcal{P}(\phi_{\text{SCm}}^{(2D)}, \phi_{\text{UA}}^{(2D)})$ applied to $\Omega_{\text{2D}}$ has dense range in $\mathcal{H}_{\text{2D}}$.

**Proof sketch**: Standard consequence of the GNS construction and the definition of $\mathcal{H}_{\text{2D}}$ as the completion of polynomials in the fields applied to the vacuum. Immediate from the reconstruction.

**Status**: Rigorously established, standard result.

## 6. The DPM vortex spectral role

The physical claim of the UQFF Phase 1 is that the **DPM vortex** — the composite operator $D^{(2D)}$ of Definition 4.1 — creates the **first excited state** above the vacuum, at energy $E_{\text{crack}}^{(2D)}$.

**Proposition 6.1** (DPM as first excited state). *Under Conjecture 5.3, and assuming the DPM composite has non-zero overlap with the first excited state, the state $D^{(2D)}(f) \Omega_{\text{2D}}$ has expectation value*

$$
\langle D^{(2D)}(f) \Omega_{\text{2D}} \mid H_{\text{2D}} \mid D^{(2D)}(f) \Omega_{\text{2D}} \rangle \geq \Delta_{\text{2D}} \cdot \| D^{(2D)}(f) \Omega_{\text{2D}} \|^2
$$

*with equality for $f$ concentrated at spatial momentum equal to the DPM formation momentum.*

**Proof sketch**: Direct consequence of the spectral condition W2 (Conjecture 5.3) applied to the state $D^{(2D)}(f) \Omega_{\text{2D}}$, which is orthogonal to $\Omega_{\text{2D}}$ by parity of the composite.

**Status**: Conjecture, contingent on Conjecture 5.3 (which is the hard step).

## 7. Numerical check: $\Delta_{\text{2D}} = ?$ eV

Under the 4D-to-2D dimensional reduction with characteristic compactification length $L_{\text{comp}}$, the 2D reduction of $E_{\text{crack}}$ becomes

$$
E_{\text{crack}}^{(2D)} = \frac{E_{\text{crack}}^{(4D)}}{L_{\text{comp}}^2}
$$

(in natural units with the compactification metric factored out).

For $L_{\text{comp}}$ chosen as the DPM coherence length, this reproduces the physical formula-level value $E_{\text{crack}}^{(4D)} = 1.118 \times 10^{-19}$ J. The precise 2D-reduced numerical value depends on the KK-mode integration and requires explicit computation (Gap G-2.1).

**Gap G-7.1**: Explicit numerical computation of $\Delta_{\text{2D}}$ from the 4D UQFF ledger with a specific $L_{\text{comp}}$ choice. This is a mechanical calculation once Gap G-2.1 is resolved.

## 9. What transfers to 3D and 4D

Successful Phase 1 completion would provide:

**For Phase 2 (3D via Hairer regularity structures)**:
- The 3D SCm-UA action is obtained by relaxing the KK-mode restriction of Gap G-2.1 to include one more spatial dimension.

**For Phase 3 (4D principal result)**:
- The 4D case requires the full non-perturbative construction on Minkowski $\mathbb{R}^{1,3}$.
- No completely-established constructive-QFT template exists for 4D interacting field theories.
- The Phase 3 challenge is essentially the same as the original Clay problem — Phases 1 and 2 provide credibility and technique but not the 4D construction itself.
- The DPM vortex composite operator structure, if verified in 2D and 3D, provides the physical mechanism connecting the UQFF Phase 3 attempt to Yang-Mills mass generation.

**Realistic timeline**: If Phase 1 succeeds (12-24 months), Phase 2 (18-30 months) follows, Phase 3 (unknowable — as with any 4D construction) becomes possible.
