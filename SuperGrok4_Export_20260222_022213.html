### Dual Parton Model (DPM)

The **Dual Parton Model (DPM)** is a phenomenological framework in high-energy particle physics used to describe **multiple hadron production** in high-energy hadron-hadron, hadron-nucleus, and nucleus-nucleus collisions. Developed in the late 1970s and refined through the 1980s–2000s, it is rooted in **Regge theory** and the **dual topological unitarity** paradigm, providing a bridge between perturbative QCD and non-perturbative soft processes. DPM successfully reproduces experimental data on particle multiplicities, rapidity distributions, transverse momentum spectra, and particle ratios across a wide range of center-of-mass energies (from a few GeV to TeV scales), making it a cornerstone for Monte Carlo event generators like **DPMJET**, **JACEE**, and extensions in **EPOS**.

#### Historical Context and Motivation
- **Origins**: Proposed by Capella, Kaidalov, and others (e.g., Capella et al., *Physics Reports* 236, 225 (1994)) as an extension of the **multi-Pomeron exchange model**. It incorporates the **AGF (Andersson-Giovannini-Feldman) cut calculus** for multiple Reggeon exchanges.
- **Key Insight**: At high energies, collisions are dominated by **diffractive dissociation** and **multi-chain fragmentation**, where the interacting hadrons break into valence quark-diquark systems that fragment independently via **color flux tubes** (string fragmentation, akin to the Lund model).
- **Successes**: Predicts the **KNO scaling** for multiplicity distributions, **Feynman scaling** violations, and **rapidity gaps** in diffractive events. Validated against data from ISR, UA5, CDF, ALICE, and LHC experiments.

#### Core Mechanism

- **Multi-Chain Production**: Multiple Pomeron exchanges create **k sea quark-antiquark pairs**, leading to **2k+2 fragmentation chains** (two from valence systems + 2k from seas).
